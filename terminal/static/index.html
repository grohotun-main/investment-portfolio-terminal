› How the harvest scan reads — and what it cannot see

Not tax advice. This is a scan of a reconstructed ledger, not a recommendation to sell anything.

A "clear" chip is bounded by what the ledger can see. The wash guard knows only ingested trades — the strip above shows how much of the 31-day window is observed. Trades after the frontier, including this week's, are invisible.

Matching is same-instrument only. Only the same instrument key blocks — "substantially identical" ETFs are deliberately not judged, so a block can be missed. Options are outside the lot ledger and never block.

Reconstructed lots are not broker lot IDs. The ledger recovers basis, never lot identity — these rows cannot be handed to a broker as lot instructions.

An IRA blocker is permanent. An IRA replacement destroys the loss outright instead of deferring it into the new lot's basis; those rows are flagged separately, not as "wait out the window".

Only reconstructed-evidence lots are scanned. Printed-basis lots carry no arithmetic of ours to stand behind — excluded and counted, not shown.

› How the exit rule was chosen

The rule. Sell each put when its market value reaches 3× the premium paid — a profit-take that banks the convex spike in a crash instead of round-tripping it back to zero on the recovery.

How we got 3×. Back-tested exit multiples {2×, 3×, 5×, 7×} on a continuously-laddered 90-DTE / 5%-OTM SPY put program, repricing every leg against real Polygon daily option closes, scored by payoff per dollar of premium drag (crash payoff captured ÷ premium bled in calm periods).

Data. Real SPY option closes back to 2016 (Massive/Polygon Options Advanced), across five crashes: 2018 Q4 (−20%), 2020 COVID (−34%), 2022 H1 (−23%), April 2025 (−19%), Iran war 2026 (−9%).

Result — 3× wins on both axes (least drag and most payoff per premium dollar):

multdrag %/yrpay / $ drag
2×3.302.45
3×2.163.96
5×4.641.92
7×4.851.63

Higher multiples bleed more premium waiting for a bigger pop that rarely beats taking 3× early. An earlier 2-year sample wrongly favored 5× — one lucky fire made pay/$drag look like 21 — and the deeper data corrected it.

Caveat. A back-test on five episodes with option history to 2016; re-run scratch/sweep_exit_multiplier_phase2.py if the option data is ever extended further back.

› How this tab works

Position parsing. Strike + expiry come from the position description (JPM) or the most recent matching BUY transaction (Fidelity). Cost basis is recovered from BUY txns in the same account, aggregated across tranches. Code at parsers/option_positions.py.

IV + spot snapshot. Refresh option IV calls parsers/fetch_option_position_iv.py, which pulls one Polygon chain snapshot per unique (underlying, expiry) and writes the matching contract's mid, IV, Δ, and bid/ask — plus the same-expiry ATM IV (closest same-type strike to spot, no extra fetch) — to data/option_position_snapshot.csv. The CSV is the single source of truth for everything below the table.

Greeks. Δ, Γ, vega, θ are computed by our LR-American binomial pricer (parsers/options_pricer.py) using (Polygon spot, Polygon IV, fresh DTE, latest 3-mo T-bill r, fixed q per ticker). LR was chosen over CRR because vega oscillates non-monotonically across n on CRR — see parsers/stress_hedge.py docstring.

Aggregate exposure. Top row is the book at a glance, over the live book only (expired and zero-quantity legs excluded): Notional protected (Σ put contracts × 100 × strike — the face value the puts would put), Premium at risk (live Polygon-mid value of the open long options, statement value where no mid yet), Unrealized P&L (live MV − cost), and Weighted DTE (MV-weighted days to expiry). Bottom row turns per-row Greeks (raw pricer units) into book totals: Γ ($, per 1% spot) = Σ qty·100·γ·spot²·0.01, the convexity bump in $-delta from a 1% move; Vega ($, per 1 vol pt) = Σ qty·100·vega·0.01; Θ ($/day) = Σ qty·100·θ/365; and Weighted IV, MV-weighted.

Portfolio composition. Splits the filtered book into equity, cash-equivalent (SGOV / money-market / Treasuries) and existing options. Classification and the engine's input frames are built in parsers/options_hedging_data.py — unit-tested, so the data-shaping is locked.

Mode & target. A — Cap drawdown targets a maximum portfolio loss (the 5–25% radio); B — Tail hedge spends a fixed annual premium budget on deep-OTM (20%) puts instead. The target sets strike depth: Mode A strikes at the cap (10% cap → a 90%-of-spot put), Mode B at a fixed 20% OTM. Mode A nets your existing puts (recommends only the shortfall to the cap); Mode B does not — its basket is a standing premium laddered in on top of whatever you already hold. New-put tenor is ~180 days.

Scenarios. Five fixed portfolio drawdowns (−5% to −25%). For each, the engine inverts the drawdown into the implied SPY drop that would cause it, using each holding's crash-beta — the median of (name return / SPY return) across four historical crashes (COVID, 2022 H1, April 2025, the 2026 Iran war) — plus your cash cushion (parsers/crash_betas.py, parsers/hedge_scenarios.py). It then reprices everything at that stressed spot. The columns are colour-grouped: Unhedged P&L (no hedges); the blue existing-hedge group — Existing-hedge payoff (intrinsic of held puts) and Existing-hedge P&L = Unhedged P&L + that payoff (the result with only your current puts), in $ and %; New-hedge payoff (intrinsic of the recommended puts); and the green combined group — Combined hedged P&L (unhedged + both) in $ and %, plus Capital saved by hedges (existing + new). A — in a column means crash-beta history is missing for a name; refresh daily prices.

The recommendation. parsers/hedge_recommender.py finds your excess-MCR names — those whose marginal contribution to risk (the same EWMA-Ledoit-Wolf MCR as the Risk Contribution tab) exceeds 1.5× their natural weight in SPY. It sizes single-name puts to neutralize each such name's idiosyncratic loss in the worst scenario (whole contracts, rounded up), then one SPY put to fill the residual gap to your cap. Live premiums come from a targeted Polygon chain fetch (parsers/fetch_targeted_chain.py). Mode B instead splits the premium budget between SPY and the excess names by risk share, flooring contracts to the budget.

Caps. The headline reports the signed worst of the 5 scenarios — the most-negative net P&L (a real loss), not the biggest-magnitude one. Current = with existing puts only; Target = the cap you picked; Combined = after the recommended new puts. Because single-name puts neutralize concentration in full rather than trimming exactly to the cap, the combined basket over-covers — so its worst case lands well below target and, in the deeper scenarios, is often a convex gain rather than a loss (shown as +% in the table).

Existing puts & rolling. The read-only table shows each held put with its worst-case payoff and a Sell at (3×) target. The locked exit closes a put once its value reaches 3× the premium paid (shown per-option in both the existing- and new-puts tables); Roll by = 90 days before expiry; Roll into ≈ = a fresh ~180-day put dated around then (nearest listed monthly). Also roll if SPY drifts ±10% from the strike anchor — whichever first; don't sell into a drawdown.

Coverage. TEST-* accounts are scrambled fixture data and are excluded by the broker filter. The hedging math covers only held names with daily-price history (the coverage-% caption flags the gap); unpriced names and unparsed positions are dropped before the math runs.
